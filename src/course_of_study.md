# A Course of Study

A *curriculum* is an engineered structure of information. I understand two types of curriculum: an *imposed curriculum*, which provides resources, a relational framework, expected outputs, grading criteria, etc. in a self-contained package, and a *research curriculum*, which is more of a set of navigational aids, aesthetic criteria, and standards for the production of records, artifacts, and products.

A student is to be forced through an imposed curriculum, fairly rigidly. You can guide yourself through imposed curricula, which is an important skill. The purpose of following an established course of study is to be exposed to structured ways of thinking that have been fruitful in the past. In the context of a community of learners, there are several important outcomes of maintaining imposed curricula: The intersection between the languages of any two individuals is nonempty. Time is not wasted "rediscovering" established ideas. Systems that work well can be maintained efficiently and without reverse engineering or refactoring.

A research curriculum is fundamentally different in that the relevant information is not curated beforehand by an authority. The landscape of information is more "dangerous" (though I hesitate to use such an inflammatory term). Rabbit holes, apathy, delusion, conspiracy, bias, and general disorderliness comprise some of the perils encountered in the depths of research. Each of these is an unrestrained case of a tendency which has merit in moderation.

Assignment 1: Name, and explain the merit of, the restrained cases.
